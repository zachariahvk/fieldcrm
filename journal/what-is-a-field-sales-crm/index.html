What is a field sales CRM, and how do you pick one in 5 minutes?

4 min read29 September 2026By Codelash

A field sales CRM is customer relationship management software built for teams that sell in person: salespeople who visit dealers, distributors, contractors, factories and shops. It captures leads, visits, calls, deals and quotations from a phone, in the minutes between appointments, and turns them into numbers a manager can trust.

Most CRMs were designed around a person at a desk with a keyboard and an inbox. Field selling breaks that assumption in three places, and a CRM that ignores them ends up empty. This article covers those three places, a five-minute test to run in any vendor demo, and how to roll a CRM out so your salespeople actually use it.

Why a desk CRM struggles on the road

The moment of entry

At a desk, data goes in during the call. In the field, it goes in between two visits or it doesn't go in at all. If logging a visit takes longer than the walk back to the vehicle, it gets pushed to “tonight”, and tonight it loses to dinner.

Where leads come from

Desk CRMs are built for web forms and email. Field leads arrive as visiting cards, shop signboards, trade show conversations and referrals. A field CRM has to turn a photo of a card into a record, and check for duplicates as it goes.

Trust

A manager of a field team has one question a desk CRM never has to answer: did this visit actually happen? Without a way to verify visits, the data gets second-guessed, and data that's second-guessed stops being used.

The five-minute test

Feature lists all look the same. Instead, ask for five things in any demo, on a phone, and time them.

  1. Capture a visiting card. Hand over a real card. It should become a record with the right fields in well under a minute, and the CRM should flag it if the company already exists.
  2. Log a visit in a sentence. Say “met the purchase head, discussed pricing, demo next week”. You want the outcome and a follow-up task set from that sentence, not a form to fill in.
  3. Find today's follow-ups. One tap to see what is due and what is overdue. If it takes a report, salespeople won't look.
  4. Build a quote with tax. For Indian businesses that means the GST split on your letterhead, numbered, with a way to control discounts.
  5. Ask the pipeline a question. “Which deals are closest to closing?” A dashboard or a plain-language answer both count; an Excel export doesn't.

One bonus check: open it on the oldest phone anyone in your team carries. If it's slow there, it'll be slow for the people who matter most.

What a field sales CRM costs

Pricing follows one of two models. Many CRMs charge per user per month, which is simple to start but grows with every hire, and often adds charges for reporting or integrations. Others price by team, covering a set number of users for a fixed amount.

Beyond the licence, count what isn't on the pricing page: setup fees, training time, paid customisation, and the cost of a CRM that nobody fills in. A cheap CRM that sits empty is the most expensive option. Codelash Field CRM uses team plans billed annually, with no per-user add-ons; see plans and pricing.

Rolling it out without a revolt

  1. Start with capture and logging. Card scanning and one-sentence logs first. Quotes and reports can follow once the habit sticks.
  2. Agree a short outcome list together. Five or six outcomes your team actually uses beat twenty nobody reads.
  3. Import what you have on day one. An empty CRM feels like extra work. Bring your existing customer sheet in first.
  4. Run the weekly review from the CRM. When the manager uses the data, salespeople see why it matters. When the review still runs from memory, logging stops.

In short

A field sales CRM is a CRM designed around the phone and the gaps between visits. Judge it on how fast a salesperson can capture a lead and log a visit, whether a manager can trust what's logged, and whether it runs on the phones your team already carries. The five-minute test tells you more than any feature list.

Frequently asked questions

Is a field sales CRM the same as field service software?

No. Field service software schedules technicians for installations and repairs. A field sales CRM is for people who sell: it tracks leads, visits, deals and quotes for salespeople who meet customers in person.

Does a five-person team need a CRM?

If more than one person sells and the business depends on follow-ups, yes. The pain usually starts when a salesperson leaves and their customers' history leaves with them, or when the owner can't tell which deals are real.

App or browser?

Either can work if it's fast on the phones your team already has. A browser-based CRM that installs to the home screen skips app store installs and updates, which helps on shared or older phones.
