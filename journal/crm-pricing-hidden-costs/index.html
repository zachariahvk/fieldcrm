How to read a CRM pricing page before it reads you.

The price at the top of a CRM pricing page is rarely what a small sales team ends up paying. The real cost of a CRM sits in eight places: how seats are counted, the billing term, onboarding fees, the jump between tiers, add-ons, AI credits, a second tool for the field, and what it costs to leave. This checklist walks through each one, with the question to ask the vendor, so you can compare quotes on what you'll actually pay in year one.

Prices below are list prices as reported in October 2026, mostly by price-tracking sites rather than vendors themselves. They change often. Use them as ranges, and check the current page before you decide. They're in US dollars, as most vendors publish them; rupee figures are at about ₹95 to the dollar, the rate in September 2026. Many vendors price differently for India, so ask for the rupee price.

Why the headline price misleads

CRM pricing pages are built to win the first click. The number in large type is usually the cheapest paid plan, billed annually, per user. General small-business CRMs start at roughly $9 to $15 per user per month (roughly ₹850 to ₹1,400) at that level, and one popular option has a free plan for a single user.

That entry plan is real, but it's often not the plan a team with a sales manager and five salespeople ends up on. The features a growing team needs tend to sit a tier or two higher, or in paid add-ons. That's not a trick. It's how most CRM pricing works, and it's why you should price the plan you'll use rather than the one in large type.

The 8-point checklist

1. How are users counted?

Per-user (per-seat) pricing is the most common model. It's simple to start, but the bill grows with every hire. Check three things: whether there's a minimum number of seats, whether you pay for inactive users (a salesperson who left in March, a manager who logs in once a month), and whether view-only access for the owner or accounts team costs a full seat. Some field tools set seat minimums.

The other model is team pricing: one price for a set number of users. It's predictable, but check the user limit and what happens when you cross it.

Ask: “If we have six salespeople and one manager who only reads reports, how many seats do we pay for?”

2. Monthly or annual, and how do you get out?

Annual billing is normal and usually cheaper per month. That's fine if you know the exit terms. Some tools reportedly require a 12-month minimum paid upfront, and some quote annual prices in large type with a higher monthly rate in small print. One field tool lists $58 a month per user billed annually, and $69 billed monthly.

Ask: “What's the minimum term, is it paid upfront, and what notice do we give to cancel or reduce seats?”

3. Is onboarding optional?

This is the line item most likely to be missing from the pricing table. At one large CRM, the mid-tier sales plan comes with a mandatory onboarding fee of $1,500, rising to $3,500 for the top tier, about ₹1.4 lakh and ₹3.3 lakh. That's a one-time cost, but for a five-person team it can equal months of licences.

Paid onboarding isn't wrong. A heavy system may genuinely need it. But you should know about it before you budget, not after.

Ask: “Is there a setup, onboarding or implementation fee, and is it mandatory on this plan?”

4. Which tier do you actually need?

List the five things your team must do every week, then find the cheapest tier that does all five. The gaps between tiers can be steep. At one large CRM, the sales plan goes from $15 per seat on the entry tier to roughly $90 to $100 per seat on the next one. Features a field sales manager tends to want, like automation and proper reporting, often sit in the higher tiers.

Ask: “Which plan includes our must-haves, and what's the per-user price on that plan, not the entry plan?”

5. What's sold as an add-on?

Marketplaces with hundreds or thousands of add-on apps are a strength of the big CRMs. They're also where costs creep in. Watch for anything that should be core for a field team (mobile features, quotations, reporting) turning out to be a separate app with its own fee.

Ask: “Which of our must-haves need a paid add-on or a third-party app?”

6. How is AI billed?

AI features are increasingly billed separately from the seat, in credits or per action. One large vendor publishes $500 per 100,000 credits, about 20 credits (roughly $0.10, or about ₹10) per standard AI action. That can be good value, but it makes the bill depend on usage, which is hard to predict in your first year.

Ask: “Are AI features included in the seat price, capped, or billed per use? What does a team our size typically spend?”

7. Do you need a second tool for the field?

General CRMs are often weak on the road, so field teams add a route-planning, mapping or field app on top. Those tools typically cost $25 to $69 per user per month (roughly ₹2,400 to ₹6,600), and most of them sync into a CRM rather than replacing it. That means two subscriptions, two logins, and a sync to keep working.

Ask: “Can our salespeople do everything in the field from this app, or will we need another tool on top?”

8. What does it cost to leave?

Nobody budgets for leaving, but it's part of the price. Check how long you keep access to your data after cancelling. One large vendor's terms give 30 days. Check what format the export comes in, and whether customisations come with you (they usually don't).

Ask: “After we cancel, how long can we export our data, in what format, and is there a fee?”

A year-one cost worksheet

Add these up for each CRM on your shortlist, for the plan you'd actually use.

LineHow to work it out
LicencesPrice per user on the right tier × users you'll pay for × 12
OnboardingOne-time fee, if any
Add-onsEach paid add-on or app × users × 12
AIExpected credits or usage, if billed separately
Field toolSecond tool per user × field salespeople × 12, if needed
Your timeHours to set up and train × what an hour of the owner's or manager's time is worth

Illustrative only, using reported list prices: six users on a mid-tier plan at about $90 a seat is about $6,480 a year (about ₹6.2 lakh). Add a $1,500 mandatory onboarding fee and year one is close to $8,000 (about ₹7.6 lakh) before any add-ons or GST, against $1,080 (about ₹1 lakh) for the same six users on a $15 entry tier. Neither number is wrong. They're just different plans. Make sure you compare the plan you'll really use.

Buying from India: three more lines

If your business is in India, add these to the worksheet.

  • GST on top. Software subscriptions attract GST, currently 18%, and the headline price almost never includes it. A vendor registered in India adds it to the invoice. With a foreign vendor that isn't, your business usually pays it under reverse charge. Either way, budget for it, and ask your accountant whether you can claim it back.
  • A GST invoice with your GSTIN. To claim input tax credit, you need a proper tax invoice that shows your GSTIN. Ask for one before you pay, especially from a vendor that bills from abroad.
  • Dollar billing. A plan priced in dollars costs more in rupees whenever the rupee weakens, and banks usually add a foreign-exchange markup to international card payments.

Ask: “Is the price in rupees, does it include GST, and will we get a GST invoice with our GSTIN?”

In short

A CRM's real price is the plan you'll actually use, times the users you'll actually pay for, plus onboarding, add-ons, AI usage, any second tool for the field, and the cost of getting out. Ask the eight questions, fill in the worksheet, and compare year-one totals rather than headline prices. If you're still choosing what kind of CRM you need, start with what a field sales CRM is.

Frequently asked questions

What hidden costs should I expect with a CRM?

The usual ones are mandatory onboarding fees, paying for inactive or minimum seats, features locked in higher tiers, paid add-ons, AI billed per use, and a second app for field salespeople. Ask about each before you sign.

Is per-user or flat team pricing better for a small sales team?

Per-user is cheaper if you're one or two people and growing slowly. Team pricing is more predictable once you have several salespeople, as long as the user limit fits your plans.

Is annual CRM billing worth it?

Usually, if you're confident in the tool. It's typically cheaper per month. Before committing, check the minimum term, whether it's paid upfront, and how to reduce seats.

Why do some CRMs not show prices?

Some vendors price by team size or region, or are still setting prices, and prefer to quote directly. It's not a red flag on its own. Ask for the full year-one cost in writing.
